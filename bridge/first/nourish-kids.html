A possible first table

1. The caregiver chooses

What is a practical meal? What does the caregiver freely choose to disclose? No public child name, dietary/medical details, personal address or family history.

2. A steward responds voluntarily

An actually authorized local food worker could review one selected requirement. A proposed slip is not an actual request sent, a resource offer or a confirmed commitment.

3. Someone independently verifies the result

Only real, safe, consented action and recipient confirmation can establish usefulness. Missing and unknown are not automatic proof of need, and a digital receipt is not food.

4. People may create another table

The successor chooses its own rules and may decline. No membership, app login, faith practice or founder approval is required.

What could funding actually do?

Pay resident co-designers and qualified access/food-safety reviewers. Cover actual meals, access and transport for permitted gatherings. Do not pay institutional leaders for status or assert partnerships they have not authorized. Music and creative tables remain other voluntary first doors.
