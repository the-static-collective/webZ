Fiction — not evidence of occurrence

The machine in the story.

In a Jubilee Engine story, a traveler hunts for a machine made from rare parts. The pieces turn out to be evidence of relationships that cannot be manufactured. Fiction makes a question legible; it does not prove the machine or its relationships already exist.

Prototype — a test is not a field outcome

A small working crossing.

Books, manga, and music generated design questions. Some became executable software, including an early caregiver-owned meal-gap source and a separate receiving HOLD. Synthetic tests examine bounded code. They do not prove a meal was delivered or that a family consented.

Human field — independent results not yet proved

The stranger is not a character.

Conversations about groceries, faith, music, and mutual support are beginning. A proposed six-month experiment asks whether a useful invitation could become a table that people freely steward—and whether a second table could be independently started. Refusal is an authentic answer.

The recursive reel

The plot can revise its next scene. It cannot revise its evidence.

WITNESS→STORY→POSSIBLE WORLD→PROTOTYPE→ENCOUNTER→NEW WITNESS

A song can precede a prototype. A prototype can expose a problem in the imaginary world. A stranger's refusal can be the most important new evidence.

The invitation is real only when offered. The relationship is real only when independent people make it. The next chapter belongs to whoever lives it.

One proposed field experiment

Could there be a second table?

Start with a concrete, low-pressure need: food, useful work, or a shared story. Invite willing people to decide what they want, whether anything should continue, and how they wish to be represented. Compensate co-design where possible; design for access and safe refusal.

This page does not enroll anyone, store data, book events, or offer a staffed service. It is a public-safe narrative doorway, not evidence that the experiment has happened.
