01 / TWO VERY DIFFERENT CLOCKS

Heat this winter.
Wood for next winter.

READY NOW · VERIFY EVERY BUNDLE

Dry, seasoned, legal-to-move wood.

Already split and dried firewood is the only plausible immediate donation source. A moisture meter reading below 20% is best; the wood still needs legal source, species, pest movement, receiving-site and appliance checks.

Immediate shortage? Start with existing energy-assistance providers and authorized fuel vendors. Our unverified drafts are not emergency dispatch.

BUILD FOR 2027 / FUTURE HEATING SEASON

Dead trees, fresh logs and surplus wood.

Newly cut wood generally needs many months of seasoning; a standing dead tree is not guaranteed ready to burn. Qualified insured arborists or trained crews must assess hazardous tree work, and landowners must authorize any removal.

Recover safe local wood, split and stack it appropriately, test after drying, and carry lawful inventory into future winters.

02 / IMMEDIATE RELIEF FIRST

Someone needs heat?
Don't give them a promise.

Give them an existing route that can actually process heating assistance while we build the firewood network.

Minnesota Energy Assistance Program

The state says eligible households can receive help with bills and payments to fuel providers—including wood. Application deadline: May 31, 2027. On October 26, 2026, the Department of Commerce announced approximately $111 million in federal funding for the heating season.

03 / THE COMMUNITY WEAVE

One tree is not a delivery.

01

Notice

Wood supply, paid tree work, available dry stock, storage space, fuel vendors, or a need recognized by a trusted assistance organization.

02

Classify

Ready-this-winter fuel versus green wood for a future season. Never claim a dead yard tree is heat-ready.

03

Verify

Tree ownership, professional tree care, species and moisture, pests/quarantine, vehicles, insurance and steward approval.

04

Match

Local nonprofit or authorized fuel provider receives a bounded offer privately and may accept or refuse independently.

05

Deliver

Only approved, lawful and appropriately insured handling, with recipient consent; no unsafe volunteer tree work.

06

Witness

Record only the real result: amount verified, a completed authorized delivery, a refusal, or an unresolved problem.

04 / WHO COULD SHARE THE WORK?

Lots of useful roles.
No forced partnership.

Churches

Willing volunteer coordinators, dry storage, meals and neighbor care—without faith requirements for aid.

Colleges

Consented adult service-learning in inventory, moisture testing, mapping, logistics, energy efficiency, accessibility or public-interest design.

Schools

Optional school-approved environmental education or communications only, with safeguarding. No student tree felling, chainsaw operation, delivery to strangers or access to beneficiary records.

Tree professionals

Licensed or insured as applicable; assess hazardous trees, material health and proper handling. No assumption of donated labor.

Community organizations

Independently screen and coordinate fuel need, distribute relief information, and control private recipient details.

Neighbors and growers

Voluntary safe local surplus, equipment, money for authorized fuel vendors, and covered dry storage.

06 / STOP-LINES · NO EXCEPTIONS

Don't move pests.
Don't move danger.

MN DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE

Emerald ash borer and other quarantines restrict moving uncertified hardwood firewood out of regulated areas. Favor local supply. Use certified heat-treated wood for cross-quarantine movements only when labeling and current rules permit.

Firewood movement rules ↗
US ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION AGENCY

Burn clean, dry, seasoned untreated wood. Under 20% moisture is best. Maintain wood stoves and chimneys; install working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms. Smoke affects neighbors and people with respiratory conditions.

Wood heat safety ↗
STATE FOREST / PUBLIC LAND

Do not assume downed wood belongs to volunteers. Personal-use wood taken home from a Minnesota state forest requires a permit, and state park firewood collection is generally prohibited.

Minnesota DNR rules ↗

No personal data storage or submission, no public-address map, no donor-to-recipient direct matching, no underage dangerous tool use, no promises of winter-ready wood, no assumed cross-county transport permission.
