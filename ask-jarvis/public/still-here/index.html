01 / STEADY

Just the next hour.

Don't decide the rest of your life tonight. Pick the smallest action that makes the next hour safer or less isolated.

Safety is the first practical question. If you or a child may be physically harmed, prioritize distance from the threat and a safer place or a trusted nearby person. Emergency services are an option where appropriate; this page is not monitored and cannot summon help. If you might hurt someone, leave the confrontation and seek immediate in-person support rather than escalating it.
  1. Put some distance between yourself and any confrontation. Do not send the angry message or make a public accusation tonight.
  2. Choose one trustworthy person you could contact without explaining everything.
  3. Preserve anything time-sensitive, but leave interpretation and decisions for later.
  4. Eat, drink water, rest if possible. These are practical actions, not a substitute for help.

This is a personal worksheet, not a conversation with a responder. Nothing is sent on your behalf.

04 / THE ROAD TO YOUR CHILD

Protect the relationship, not the argument.

You can pursue clarity about your child without escalating a conflict with your co-parent. You can also seek legal information without committing to years of litigation.

Child's whereabouts unknown? Separate what you know from your hypothesis. If there's credible evidence of immediate danger or abduction, contact law enforcement. Otherwise, urgently ask a qualified family-law resource how parentage, custody orders, relocation, jurisdiction, and safe contact apply to your specific situation. Do not use a third party to circumvent a protective order.

Three conversations worth preparing

  1. With a trusted person: “I need someone who can listen without deciding who is right, and help me make one practical plan.”
  2. With a legal self-help service: “What low-cost steps can protect my parenting relationship? Are there fee waivers, pro se forms, or urgent temporary remedies? What deadlines matter?”
  3. With a prosecutor or victim advocate, if testimony is being pressured: “I want to report a specific statement I believe was intended to influence testimony. Here is what was said, who heard it, and what I can document.”

How we protect the person arriving

We don't adjudicate allegations, diagnose people who aren't here, or publish private stories. A personal draft isn't proof; a source audit isn't a verdict. No account, remote intake, database storage, or automated matching is operating here. A local draft can still be exposed by a monitored device, a download, a clipboard, or printing. Real peer matching must wait for trained moderation, consent, and a safe reporting process.

One particular, one action, one return. We begin with fathers who have survived abuse. Mothers, grandparents, friends, and anyone who needs a human road back are welcome. No forced disclosures. No required registration. No promise we cannot keep.
