## Project Description (723/750; source of truth: `bridge/first/voqal-people-budget.mjs`)
I'm a father trying to make a world my daughter can inherit and enjoy living in—and that other people can help shape. Still Here: The Second Table tests whether a meal, song or practical task can help isolated neighbors connect, complete something useful and begin a group they govern. I've joined the Coalition of Care core team in southern Minnesota and met local leaders in food access, co-ops, philanthropy and higher education; none has endorsed this project. The fellowship would fund accessible gatherings, fairly paid resident co-design, independent safety review and honest evidence of what works. Participation, faith and music are optional. A second table must be free to change the method or refuse its founder.

## Impact: What's Possible (max 10,000; draft for applicant revision)

As a father, I want to help make a world my daughter can enjoy living in, not simply endure. That aspiration is larger than my family: I want a community where a person who has lost their network does not need a résumé, an account, a membership fee, a working phone, a compelling personal tragedy, or the approval of a central organization simply to contribute something useful. A table can be an ordinary meal, a repair question, an art night or a small act of land care. Its purpose is not attendance. It is to make human cooperation possible and to give participants the ability to carry it somewhere else without asking the founder.

I am starting from a falsifiable question: can a welcoming first circle lead to one actually completed, independently confirmed useful action and an **independent second circle**? The second circle must be able to change the method, refuse an invitation, decide its own rules, or close without penalty. It cannot be a franchise governed by my permission or by a software account. The point is redistribution of practical power.

Conventional support can make access depend on paperwork, English fluency, transportation, social reputation, internet access, organizational referrals, and the capacity to disclose private histories repeatedly. Those burdens do not fall evenly. This experiment will invite people who face those barriers into **paid co-design**, rather than treating them only as recipients or testimonials. In practical terms I will budget for transportation, childcare, language and disability accommodations; commission independent safety and access reviews; seek free and physically accessible locations; and avoid publishing participant case histories. People may decline without being counted as failures.

My long-term goal is not a single platform or a leader whom everyone needs. In twenty years, I would want communities to hold their own locally revised practices: a portable invitation, a record of the work they consent to share, and an honest right to reject what they inherit. If the method reproduces extraction, founder dependency, unsafe practices or inaccessible gatherings, participants and independent reviewers must be able to stop it.

## Project Summary: What You'll Do (max 7,500; draft for applicant revision)

The six-month project is an experiment in actual relationships, not a software launch.

**April 2027: Listen and protect.** Seek permission from appropriate local hosts and conveners to review the idea; commission independent accessibility and safeguarding critique; compensate voluntary community co-designers; identify a small, practical task that people want, not a problem chosen for them. Draft no-account paper invitations, accessible information and a method for recording both acceptance and refusal without collecting personal testimonies.

**May–June: Meet and return.** Attempt one accessible community table and one short resource-steward conversation per week when hosts and participants choose to do so. Use free existing rooms when possible. Pay the real costs of accessible participation and modest food, but no access fees to institutional leaders. Each encounter asks who owns a resource, whether a useful action is genuinely authorized, and who can independently confirm that it happened. A task not completed remains an offer or a plan, not an outcome.

**Music as an optional doorway.** A participant-led singalong, original-song workshop, shared rhythm or low-pressure music/story station can help a newcomer do something with others before being asked to tell a personal story. I can contribute guitar and original songwriting, but the objective is cooperation, not audience size, releases or a festival. No one needs to sing, adopt a faith, or use an app to participate; host permission, rights and accessible nonmusic alternatives are required.

**July–August: Test ownership.** Support participant-designed potlucks or creative evenings when useful, and conduct a small permitted rehearsal gathering only if safety, venue, personnel and cost conditions permit. Invite, never manufacture, a second table. Its members must choose their own rules, records, hosting arrangements and whether to continue. Test a scenario where I stop facilitating; measure whether anything useful survives without founder approval.

**September: Return and report.** Share results, refusals, accessibility failures, paid participation costs and independent feedback. If a fully permitted and separately funded family festival at Harmony Park is viable, it may celebrate participant-owned work; the $5,000 planning seed alone is not its budget. Otherwise conclude at a smaller approved gathering. No venue, partner, resident or fellowship guest is presently confirmed.

**Success conditions:** an actual host authorizes the activity; a willing participant chooses an action; the person or resource owner confirms completion and usefulness; and a successor can independently adapt or decline the founding method. Number of attendees, signed synthetic keys and generated creative artifacts are not substitutes for these conditions. Publish only consented, non-sensitive aggregate lessons.

**Equal disability access and representation:** disabled participants must be invited into paid design and actual decisions, not only offered a seat. Reserve budget for transport, childcare, accessible communication, interpretation and adjustable event formats; obtain independent specialist review before claiming accessibility. Where a host cannot safely provide access, change the venue or do not run the activity.

## Resources and Support: What Do You Need (max 5,000; draft)

I need structured learning from people who already understand resident-led organizing, participant compensation, disability justice, safeguarding, cooperative governance and practical small-event production. I especially want coaching on how to avoid founder dependency and how to ask permission without turning trusted institutions into gatekeepers. Voqal's coaching and cohort would provide independent challenge outside the local circles. ## Idea Origin: What Is the Lineage (max 5,000; first-person applicant-review draft)

I'm a dad before I'm a technologist, musician or organizer. I want to help make a world my daughter can inherit and **enjoy living in**. That means more than safety. I want her to find neighbors who can feed one another, music and laughter, room to make things, and communities she can join and reshape without having to ask a founder's permission.

This isn't an idea I discovered while preparing a grant application. Looking back through my earlier work, the same question appears in small, concrete places. In 2024 I was asking practical questions about infant feeding and ways for generations of a family to share memories without needing technical confidence. In 2025 I began developing a children's board-book series, starting with *You Are Wonderfully Made*, to give young children language for dignity, kindness and purpose. I kept writing songs and creating music, increasingly interested in what a voice or an unfinished song could leave for someone who comes later. I want to pass on conditions in which my daughter can grow, not dictate the shape her life must take.

Music taught me something about making room. I began with drums and now play guitar and write songs, including in a worship ensemble. When the music has a steady pulse, other musicians can enter with their own parts. The community experiment uses a related idea: a low-pressure starting point such as a meal, song, repair or creative workshop, followed by a chance to do something useful together. Participation in music or faith is optional. The point isn't an audience; it's belonging and agency.

I have worked with technology, web design and collaborative creative work, and more recently helped build experimental open-source tools through The Static Collective. Those prototypes can demonstrate portable methods and rights to refuse, but they have not created verified human communities. I want the real-world tests to be governed by the people who actually participate.

In October 2026 I joined the Greater Mankato Coalition of Care core team and spoke face-to-face with five people working across community philanthropy, grassroots organizing, food access, co-ops and university technology. That gives me a beginning, not institutional endorsement or committed partners. The conversations happened without a grant. The fellowship could make sustained organizing possible while paying residents for substantive co-design and meeting real participation, access and independent safety costs.

I don't want to build a community my daughter must inherit unchanged. I want to help build a culture in which people can begin something, verify that it helps, and then hand it to others who can revise or refuse it. The Second Table is a six-month attempt to see whether that kind of inheritance is possible beyond my own family.
