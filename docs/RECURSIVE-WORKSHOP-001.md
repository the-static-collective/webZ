## The next-hands composition

An offline-first Suitcase recognizes a missing **capability** rather than assuming it already contains the complete machine.

```text
Need: hold a camera or manipulate a small workpiece
  ↓
JAR;VIS / planZ: identify ONE bounded missing capability
  ↓
GHoT: inspect declared compatible tools and operator-granted capacity
  ↓
STATIC OS: CAD source / printable part candidate / exact slicer packet
  ↓
reLATTE: separately governed owner RECEIVE → HOLD / REFUSE (no auto start)
  ↓
Human owner: authorize one specific supervised local job, when safe
  ↓
Human operator: physically print, assemble printed and nonprinted components
  ↓
Independent inspector: measure actual part and bounded operation
  ↓
PENNY / Full Measure: separately review resource custody and performed work
  ↓
GRAFT / TranchNode: candidate method → second independent physical build
  ↓
Next capability becomes eligible for a *new proposal*, not automatic self-upgrade
```

This is **partial recursive fabrication**: the workshop might print brackets, gripper shells, housings, jigs, or modular frames that enable further work. Common fused-filament 3D printers do **not** fabricate motors, bearings, power electronics, controllers, precision tooling, metal fasteners, safe wiring or all the expertise required to build an operating robot. A new physical arm additionally needs rated loads, motion/pinch safety, control, calibration, human oversight and an emergency stop. No unsafe autonomous power-on or self-replicating printer claim.

### The source-informed executable here

`bridge/mit-solve/capability-growth.mjs` constructs an offline deterministic **HOLD-only**, SHA-256 integrity-addressed candidate card for one of four goals: `arm`, `body`, `printer`, `outreach`. It records potentially printable components, required off-the-shelf hardware and qualified human work, independent tests, named original source doors, zero field proof and zero machine/economic/network effects.

```sh
node --test tests/capability-growth.test.mjs
node --input-type=module -e 'import {proposeNextCapability} from "./bridge/mit-solve/capability-growth.mjs"; console.log(JSON.stringify(proposeNextCapability("arm"),null,2))'
```

This module does **not** execute or impersonate STATIC OS, GHoT, OctoPrint, CANNON or reLATTE. Its SHA-256 digest commits local candidate bytes, not physical truth or a signature. It will not accept an unknown goal or claim hardware permission. The original source owners' actual code/receipts remain separately inspectable through the linked PRs.

## MIT Solve / Blueprint Foundry fit

**Parent:** [Living Suitcase MIT draft PR #187](https://github.com/the-static-collective/webZ/pull/187). Keep the **first** reproducibility target an ordinary safe, independently assembled local computing node; a 3D printer is a *future optional tool*, not a fabricated completed Suitcase or a requirement to make the first pilot work.

Then test one mechanically low-consequence, owner-approved 3D-printed mounting fixture or case insert. Record actual BOM, printer/material/profile, machine authorization, G-code bytes, operator, failed prints, post-print measurements, costs, whether the part was useful, and an independent attempt at reproduction. That becomes a narrow blueprint only after an unrelated operator succeeds. Robotic arm actuation is a later, separately qualified gate.

A whole machine cannot be certified by a signed design. **PRINTED PART != FUNCTIONAL ROBOT. ONE TOOL != AUTONOMOUS WORKSHOP. WORKING BUILD != REPRODUCIBLE BLUEPRINT.**

## Voqal / Still Here fit

The potential Still Here application remains **one survivor-informed first-contact and safeguarding experiment**, not a 3D-printing grant application. A physically portable kit could *optionally* produce a blank public-facing card stand, sign holder, organizer or other nonsensitive accessibility/display aid if a responsible host wants it. Paper alternatives remain sufficient; no printer or technical credentials may become a condition for receiving help.

No vulnerable person's account, legal evidence, case records, exact safe location, child's information or identifying design labels may be placed in printer files, public photos, device telemetry, this Git branch, prompts or print-shop demonstrations. A 3D fabrication mechanism cannot independently determine a trusted first human contact, run a peer support service, or certify safeguarding. A **real qualified safeguarding review** remains prior to survivor participation.

## Required field gates (not passed)

1. One real owner/host decides whether the physical workshop is appropriate; recorded consent and safe power/siting.
2. Named printer/material/firmware, verified CAD license/rights and calibrated slicer profile for one **specific harmless fixture**, not a motorized arm.
3. Supervised one-time human-machine actuation with physical safety and failure shutdown.
4. Independent dimension and function inspection; private evidence preserved without public personal information.
5. A second independent builder reproduces the part's declared function; actual labor/cost/failure baseline is compared.
6. Only then propose a bounded next capability; no automatic remote printing, robot movement, material debit, Deed or value issuance.

**The recursive mechanism is the repeatable request and evidence loop, not spontaneous manufacture.**

**Current evidence:** real source-code fabrication and proposal components; `physical_part_printed=false`, `machine_owner_authenticated=false`, `robot_assembled=false`, `independent_second_build=false`, `community_or_service_deployed=false` in this new bounded integration.
