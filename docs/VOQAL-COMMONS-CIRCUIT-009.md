# VOQAL-009 — THE COMMONS CIRCUIT / TABLE & ROAD

**October 29, 2026 · new applicant-review-only direction · NO REAL EVENTS, GRANTS, PARTNERSHIPS, RECRUITMENT, SUBMISSION OR OUTREACH AUTHORIZED BY THIS DOCUMENT.** The private applicant draft v6 remains separate and unchanged.

## The critical change

**Use the Voqal fellowship primarily to fund *human proximity and voluntary repeated interaction*, not software construction.** Over April–September 2027, the proposed fellow would be physically present at tables, meals, events, music nights, farms, church/community spaces, food distribution conversations, campuses, artist gatherings and permitted civic venues. Every week, attempt a low-pressure community encounter **and** one conversation with a steward who legitimately manages a piece of the existing culture, infrastructure or local economy.

The purpose is neither to buy influence nor to assemble prestigious names. A **steward** is a person with practical, legitimate responsibility over space, resources, relationships, programs, permissions or creative work. The people most affected by exclusion have equal authority to define needs, decline the proposed culture and govern an independent circle. Community-owned cooperation emerges only from *their* affirmative choices.

**Long horizon:** An alternative, locally governed culture and practical economy of care, creative contribution and reciprocal useful work can grow *alongside* existing institutions. The founding method, printable invitations, resource protocols and cultural artifacts can be inherited **without compulsory membership or inherited founder authority**. No specific new exchange, institutional endorsement or economic effect already exists.

**First six-month experiment:** Can weekly tables, potluck relay invitations, collective art/music activity and recurring steward meetings allow people without established networks to reach a first credible person, complete a *voluntary useful act*, and if they choose, pass an adaptable, independent community table to someone else? A useful outcome may be a rejection, referral to an existing better service, failed attempt, independent critique or one independently confirmed non-sensitive shared task.

## The repeatable week: one table + one legitimate steward + one returned result

1. **Invite without presuming a network.** Join or host one genuinely welcoming, low-cost, nonexclusive physical table (coffee, community meal, music, an existing public program). Offer paper invitations and optional personal contact choice; **no account, ideology, religious participation or public testimony required**.
2. **Hear from a steward with actual responsibility.** Independently seek one weekly conversation with someone able to assess a real constraint or authorize a limited use: a library/community programmer, church hall scheduler, food shelf steward, campus services lead, artist/venue operator, co-op/farmer organizer, festival safety lead, disability-access reviewer, or relevant public permitting staff. Their institutional title is not a partner claim or a proxy for affected residents' voices.
3. **Discover the actual joint interest.** Ask what is useful to them, what must remain off-limits, and what they already do well. A steward can decline or tell the group not to duplicate an existing working process.
4. **Try a limited real-world exchange if all parties separately authorize.** A permitted meeting space, a volunteer-run music moment, an appropriate food supply question, a community print run, a repair skill, an accessible ride or a shared equipment task; actual people choose. Do not use unverified products/food, create unlicensed services, collect survivor histories or treat a proposed resource as already available.
5. **Return evidence to the actual participants.** Ask separately whether something happened and helped; record only consented, nonidentifying learning in the public layer. They decide if a next table should exist and who owns it. No automatic onboarding or reward token.
6. **Leave an independent method, not command.** The group can change the ritual, refuse it, keep its own rules/records, stop using WebZ, or reproduce a safe public-only method in another venue with its own permissions.

The nearest thing to a weekly schedule is an **operator discipline, not an event calendar**: attempt one inclusive table and one steward conversation each week; monthly potluck anchors, four music/arts nights and two optional mini-festivals are add-on learning occasions, never prerequisites to a safe first human contact.

## Cultural events are not decorative; they are the mechanism

**Music** reduces the need to start with technical explanation or vulnerable disclosure. Group-created songs, performances and open mic collaborations can serve as invitations and inherited culture, subject to author/performer consent and rights.

**Potluck chains** pass a voluntary and low-cost hosting practice from table A to independently governed table B. No host is compelled to recruit a successor or report guest identities.

**Festivals** can briefly bring the existing local ecology into one shared space: people, performers, practical help desks run by the actual qualified providers, local producers, institutions, and art-making. A festival is not an unsafe surrogate survivor-support group, mass public data harvest, or automatic demonstration of a new economy.

**Economic experiments** begin with bounded things participants already control: space, time, food handled according to rules, tools, music, skills, transportation and mutually accepted responsibilities. One actual act can be checked by the responsible giver and receiver. PENNY/Full Measure/GrO/reLATTE are optional *representation and accounting* instruments for independently authorized real activity—never substitutes for real assets, real ownership, cash accounts or relationships.

## Authorities as **stewards**, not an elite invitation-only project

The operator's contact register should have **two equally important sources of reality**:
- Residents and people experiencing exclusion, who define what is actually missing, with paid participation and meaningful veto.
- Permission-bearing resource stewards, who may decide about specific existing locations, programs, operational rules and materials, but **cannot authorize other people's participation or dictate the new group's constitution**.

Suggested institutional *roles* to approach, without implying affiliation: facilities/program directors at campuses and libraries; community-food/co-op/farm managers; nonprofit and church space administrators; arts/venue coordinators; festival safety and production leads; accessible transport operators; municipal permitting officials (neutral, transparent logistics only); independent artists and resident organizers. At least as much attention should go to people not already in these organizations. No political candidates, parties or private officials are sought for a particular electoral outcome; public engagement is open, lawful and nonpartisan.

The first contact asks **what permission or coordination gap is real**, not for an endorsement of the entire architecture. As a relationship grows, people may voluntarily propose a shared table, a project, an organization, or a distinct local institution. Each is a separate opt-in stage.

## Six-month field sequence

**April — build trust and safeguards.** Inventory existing local initiatives without claiming partnership, ask independent safety/access reviewers to critique first-contact proposals, obtain operator/venue permissions, and host or join first small gatherings. No survivor case intake at public events.

**May–June — weekly tables and potluck relays.** Start the accessible repeating rhythm, seek weekly steward meetings, ask local participants whether a single practical resource crossing would help, and test it only after genuine permission.

**July–August — culture and economies in public.** Conditional small music/art gatherings and up to two safe mini-festival prototypes; let each independent table decide whether to repeat, adapt or stop. Test one bounded non-sensitive useful-work exchange and return its evidence to the rightful owners.

**September — independent inheritance test.** Seek third-party critique. Ask whether a willing group can retain its own rules and decline dependence on the original founder, tools or funding. Publish *only approved public-safe* methods and honest outcomes. No need to declare a new organization if none exists.

## Falsifiable outputs and anti-vanity accounting

An event count is **activity**, not social impact. One hundred attendees do not prove meaningful connection. One signed event receipt cannot certify trust. The project should report:

- **Attempts vs real meetings**: number of proposed and authorized tables, potlucks, festivals, site conversations; cancellations and explicit refusals.
- **Affected people's decision power**: paid co-designers, accessibility and safety objections, participant-chosen/modified rules, and whether they could meaningfully decline.
- **Practical returns**: receiver-confirmed real shared tasks, owner-permitted resource use, what remained unfulfilled, and which prior service worked better.
- **Relational durability**: whether people independently choose a second encounter, independent leadership or a successor event, with no identity tracing or attendance pressure.
- **Freedom to leave**: whether ownership and practical ability to operate/withdraw remain with local circles; founder absent test where safe and voluntary.
- **Economic honesty**: actual money spent, services delivered, resources consumed, paid human labor, not speculative tokens, market valuations or hypothetical barter.

**Critical falsifier:** if weekly contact only produces relationships with gatekeepers, and not genuinely voluntary connections or power for people with less institutional access, the six-month experiment has **failed its stated justice purpose**. Redirect the method accordingly.

## Boundaries that protect participants and keep the Voqal ask credible

A public festival is **not** a domestic-abuse support circle. The Still Here initial need remains isolated adults (including fathers navigating abuse/separation) without trusted networks; anyone with an identifiable safety issue needs a separately reviewed and qualified pathway, not a mass-invitation signup. Do not collect survivor disclosures at music/potlucks or publish children's data, legal evidence, survivor locations or relationship labels. Do not require participation in a church or political/religious identity to receive ordinary aid.

The original Relational Inheritance 008 code experiment independently proves key-bound succession **within its exact protocol**; the larger field experiment asks whether real people can inherit a voluntary founding practice **without inheriting its founder's social, financial or institutional control**. Actual human relationships, legal authorization and ownership must be independently reviewed.

The technical ecosystem—CANNON, JAR;VIS, reLATTE, PENNY, Full Measure, GrO, GHoT, STATIC OS—remains supporting infrastructure. **No additional software or hardware build is needed to hold the first table.** If paper cards and music deliver the encounter, use them.

**Application language guardrail:** Describe this as an *experiment in relationship-driven community power and voluntary local cultural/economic practice*, not a completed comprehensive alternative economy, already operating festival, staffed survivor service, or claimed partnership.
