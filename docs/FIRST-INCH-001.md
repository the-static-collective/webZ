# FIRST-INCH-001 — One question before the machinery

**Status:** experimental public-facing prototype; review branch only, not a verified live deployment.

## The five-second proposition

> We connect what people have with what people need, and help turn that connection into something real.

The first screen asks: **What are you trying to accomplish?**

Do not describe CANNON, JAR;VIS, reLATTE, the Git map, AI providers, receipts or internal repositories until the visitor requests the machinery. The small doorway must be useful on its own.

## Whom it serves

- **Core first users:** independent organizers, small nonprofits, churches, neighborhood projects, makers, community groups and volunteer teams that need more coordination and capacity.
- **Potential stewards:** institutions with facilities, skills, people or review capacity.
- **Potential funders:** institutions evaluating measured pilot outcomes.

These are distinct roles, not validated market adoption, endorsements, paying customers or enrolled partners.

## What the browser actually does

1. Ask for one goal. Optional fields allow someone to name resources, uncertainty and the type of work.
2. Generate a deterministic, edit-by-regenerating **draft** first work card containing a suggested small experiment, human authorization gate, and what evidence would be required afterward.
3. Let the visitor deliberately **copy**, **save text**, **print**, or **open a prefilled email draft** addressed to us@abundent.org.
4. Retain nothing in storage; make no model calls or external fetches. Email sending requires a separate action in the visitor's own mail application.

This is **not a functioning model integration** and cannot find real resources, verify participants, coordinate action, make promises or send a message itself. It is the first, intentionally low-complexity invitation into the larger open-source machine.

## Stranger test (conduct with consent; no personal or case-sensitive information)

Give someone the link without explaining the project. Ask them to try one fictional or non-sensitive goal. Observe, without collecting private data:

- Can they explain what Abundent offers after **10 seconds**?
- Can they prepare a meaningful next-step card within **60 seconds**?
- Is the output clearly labeled **suggested, unverified and unsent**?
- Would they choose to ask for help, share the draft, or try the first experiment? Why?
- What single word, question or screen is confusing?

Record only consented, non-identifying feedback. **Do not inflate a local draft into real-world proof.** Count completed consenting human trials separately from code tests, suggestions and self-declared interest.

### Follow-on experiments — not built here

- An opt-in, consent-aware JAR;VIS handoff from this draft into a bounded, source-pinned research candidate.
- An explain-on-demand view of public owners and source receipts, never a flood of internals.
- Human reviewer follow-up through existing inbox capabilities, preserving refusal/withdrawal and independent review.
- Small, verified community pilot outcomes rather than likes, visits or sheer repository count.

## One-paragraph answer to an outside evaluator

We build open-source tools that help people turn needs and available resources into small, testable pieces of useful work. Our first users are independent organizers, makers, nonprofits, churches and community groups; institutions may serve as stewards or funders. We do not train our own LLM on people's reviews. We connect existing AI and other tools, but human participants decide what actually happens. Our first practical demonstration is a one-question, local-first work-card prototype; connecting it to verified resources and people is future work, not a current claim.

**Truth boundaries:** draft ≠ plan verified; code exists ≠ deployed; sample output ≠ human consent; unmerged PR ≠ accepted release; reviewer feedback ≠ partner endorsement.
