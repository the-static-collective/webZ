## Exact research distinction

**What passes:** public operating-method description, ancestry digest, source provenance, an invitation, and possible independently adopted practices.

**What does not pass:** control over another person's private key, member records, governance rights, legal status, community membership, public discoverability, crisis/safeguarding qualification, resource commitments or the ability to withdraw another owner's current offer.

**Inheritance without authority is not isolation without responsibility.** Independent groups can still negotiate voluntary mutual care, accountable relationships, safeguarding standards and specific time-limited delegated rights. They simply cannot inherit a stranger's governance or consent from file ancestry alone. A founder can independently withdraw *their own* ongoing offers; this does not erase historical provenance or revoke someone else's separate offer. A recipient can refuse a connection without a penalty. A group can choose not to reproduce the method.

## Native executable proof, rather than a fabricated test API

This repository already has genuine locally generated **ECDSA P-256** Suitecase-001 signing and SUITCASE-002 GENESIS/SEED/CAPABILITY/WITHDRAW local calculations:

- [Original WebZ SUITCASE-001/002](../bridge/suitcase/protocol.mjs) signs public-safe packets and cryptographically verifies each issuer key, canonical digest and signature, while explicitly stating a key is *not* verified personal identity.
- [Original independent GENESIS/discover](../bridge/suitcase/foundry.mjs) ensures a descendant separately signs its own founding; it will not treat a predecessor's withdrawal as a descendant's withdrawal.
- The new [relational-inheritance.mjs](../bridge/first/relational-inheritance.mjs) **imports and executes those original functions**. It is a new bounded experiment, not a reLATTE-native receiver or a human trial.

Four **fictional** key holders, A (source), B (first circle), C (person who refuses), D (second-generation independent circle):

```text
  A: publishes method SHA-256 M
  A: P-256 signs invitation SEED_AB  [INVITATION_NOT_CONTROL]
                          │
                          ▼
  B: generates its own NONEXPORTABLE P-256 key
  B: signs independent GENESIS_B with ancestry AB and own rules
  B: signs CAPABILITY_B (public-only practice)
  A: signs a purported withdrawal of B's card  ──> ignored (A is not B)
  B: signs SEED_BD (passes the public method on) ─────────┐
                                                         ▼
  D: generates a different NONEXPORTABLE key, adopts M, *changes a local ritual*
  D: signs its own GENESIS_D and CAPABILITY_D
  B: signs a purported withdrawal of D's card  ──> ignored (B is not D)
  B: withdraws CAPABILITY_B with B's own key   ──> B disappears locally
  D: CAPABILITY_D remains discoverable        ──> D controls D

  A: separately signs bounded invitation OFFER_AC
  C: independently signs DISPOSITION REFUSE   ──> refusal is verified
```

This tests **lineage + freedom to revise + refusal + descendant non-control**, not merely whether two JSON files have different names. Eleven **actual native signed parcels** are generated and an independent cold verify operation checks their signatures, original source-method bytes, independent keyholder digests, authorized revocation and refusal. No private keys are exported; the proof artifact can be reviewed later without the original process or signer keys.

### What this proof does NOT establish

**No real human belonging is proven.** Four fictional locally generated cryptographic keys are not four independent human beings; a software key cannot establish that a person is qualified, noncoercive, physically present, competent, responsible, or safe. The simulation does not run a survivor group, publish a service, establish real relational trust, conduct participatory co-design, independently attest legal identity, confirm beneficial outcomes, enable account-free secure case intake, connect to the real RDL, or submit a Voqal application. This adapter uses the **browser Suitcase training protocol**, not reLATTE wire semantics and not an authenticated remote protocol. Separate reLATTE-native integration remains a future technical task.

**Two different proof standards must not be collapsed:**

- **Software result:** signed invitation + independently signed GENESIS + independently signed refusal + distinct owner-controlled withdrawal survives cold verification; donor authority is not inherited under this exact local discovery protocol.
- **Field hypothesis:** two genuinely independently consented people can understand, use, challenge, adapt and opt out of the practice, without practical retaliation or hidden founder dependence, under qualified safeguarding review.

The second is not inferable from the first.

## Relational Design Lab-informed practice: an independent relational review card

Rather than putting RDL's name on the software, take the *publicly described design orientation* seriously. Its themes of **belonging, freedom and responsibility**, collective data practices, contextual social systems and **proliferation rather than growth controlled by a platform** translate here into questions a real, independently selected reviewer can reject or revise:

| Relational question | Evidence to seek in a future, authorized non-sensitive human study | Invalid substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whose presence changes the relationship?** | Independently map affected people, nonparticipants, operators and outside services; ask what each gains or loses | A social graph produced by software |
| **Can the newcomer refuse without punishment?** | A participant can stop, refuse and use an existing service; examine concrete consequences | A signed REFUSE button alone |
| **Who owns the local practice and accounts?** | Receiving group's actual access to hosting, paper, records and self-governance; ability to cut the upstream connection | An inherited SHA-256 and a fresh key |

| **Who bears unseen labor and risk?** | Compensation, accessibility, privacy, safeguarding and crisis-referral capacity reviewed by qualified outsiders | Calculated volunteer/deed points |
| **Does it proliferate rather than merely scale?** | An independent group can use the method without centrally managed accounts, branding or compulsory support payment | Increasing database membership or downloads |

For the first public-only test, reviewers may use wholly fictional A/B/C/D paper roles. The *Lab itself does not review, approve, design, or own this worksheet.* If an independent relationship is ever developed, ask the Lab to critique it on its own terms rather than claiming the Lab's work has been implemented by us.

**Software's non-inheritance test is necessary but not sufficient for a social-justice claim.** It tests cryptographic control, not hidden social power through money, physical space, law, reputation, dependency or access to care.

## Field-ready falsification plan (future, subject to outside safeguarding approval)

Start **without** vulnerable participant data. Ask an independent qualified reviewer to examine a fictional paper version of A, B, C, D and identify *where real-world control could persist*: funding, physical premises, accounts, reputational pressure, crisis access, shared caregivers, software hosting, source-code updates, social influence, or compromised devices. Have a different reviewer attempt a denied withdrawal and forbidden disclosure. Record disagreements and failures.

A possible *later*, authorized field protocol asks whether two independent local operators can adapt a non-sensitive, one-page voluntary founding practice, record their separate governance and retreat/withdrawal, and continue if the original source becomes unreachable. The partner must agree in advance, and participants must be able to say the existing service is safer. For survivors, professional safeguarding review precedes contact and no identifying testimony belongs in this source code or a third-party audit.

**Human falsifiers:** a participant cannot leave safely; founder controls records after a nominal transfer; a signed invitation coerces adoption of a rule; a receiving circle cannot revise a harmful rule; printed/AI-generated lineage is mistaken for a real institutional endorsement; another person's care depends on entering the experiment. Any of these should force redesign or stop.
