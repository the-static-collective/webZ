# VOQAL-007 — STILL HERE: THE FIRST CIRCLE FOUNDRY

**October 29, 2026 · proposed strategic revision for applicant review only · NOT an authorized final application, launched support service, outreach, partnership or completed field pilot.**

## Decision: widen the horizon, not the six-month pilot

The current mainline public BRIDGE-006 desk still presents an earlier **Between Rooms / Waseca food-access** candidate. A separate privately held 2026-10-29 v6 applicant draft instead proposes **Still Here: The First Circle**—isolated adults, particularly fathers experiencing relationship abuse and family separation, as the first fellowship problem. The applicant has not approved or submitted that private text. These are distinct versions, **not two submissions**.

This proposal reframes the project as **Still Here — The First Circle Foundry**, retaining Still Here's human first-contact focus while making the systemic contribution explicit: *a reproducible, independently governable method by which someone with no trusted network can begin building real, safe cooperative capacity.*

The application should describe a liberation-oriented challenge to the architecture of exclusion, not a generic support-group directory, autonomous organization factory, crisis-response service, robot workshop, or a request to fund 89 repositories.

**Big horizon:** A person need not inherit an existing institution, paid platform, friends, credentials, a church affiliation or an organization's permission to start forming a voluntary circle. The circle's people—not the software, sponsor or founder—retain the right to shape rules, dissent, refuse, and continue independently. Future contexts might include mutual aid, food access, accessible creative participation, repair shops or community-controlled infrastructure, but **the first six months test only the first-contact and first-stewardship method**.

**Small first experiment:** An unstaffed, public-safe first door and paper/phone alternatives; first recruit qualified independent safeguarding and lived-experience reviewers with compensation and decision rights; map existing support services; co-design and independently examine one need-first, testimony-optional first-contact path; attempt one genuinely consented, supervised referral or introduction *only if risk and operational prerequisites are satisfied*. A denied pilot, a professional assessment that existing practice is better, or a finding that a technique is unsafe is an honest result.

## Why this is a radical social-justice argument

A social network, grant, legal directory or referral app usually begins after an institution has existed, a user has credentials, or someone already has contact with helpful people. **The hidden prerequisite is belonging.** The project investigates how that prerequisite can be removed *without turning people into data or creating unqualified, unsafe peer-matching*. It also questions who owns a community's records, who sets participation rules, who can challenge a decision, who can withdraw, and whether a community can run without its originator. These mechanisms can redistribute **social decision-making power**; no actual transformation is proven until affected participants can exercise those powers.

Voqal's official 2027 Fellowship [criteria](https://voqalpartners.org/fellowship/) prefer early-stage systemic alternatives, a credible learning path, and work by/alongside affected people. Its [values](https://voqalpartners.org/what-we-stand-for/) emphasize working *with* communities and building power. It asks how applicants disrupt white supremacy culture, center disability inclusion and hand off work in twenty years. **Do not equate one individual's experience with the distinct histories of racial exclusion.** Instead invite paid co-design and independent critique from people with different lived experiences and let that critique change or stop the project. The first step is earning accountability to a community, not claiming existing community endorsement.

## Recommended title and official 750-character proposed description

**Candidate title:** Still Here — The First Circle Foundry

> Still Here — The First Circle Foundry asks how people cut off from trusted networks can form new, independently governed circles of practical support. Beginning with isolated adults, especially fathers affected by relationship abuse and family separation, we will co-design a need-first, testimony-optional first contact with paid survivor advisors and qualified safeguarding reviewers. Through paper, phone and optional offline tools, we will test voluntary introductions to existing resources, real refusal and withdrawal, and independently reviewed outcomes. The long-term goal is a reproducible founding method communities can own, change, and carry forward without our permission or dependence on a platform.

**713 characters**, including spaces. The applicant alone decides whether this wording accurately describes their intentions and approves any personal disclosure.

**Candidate Voqal program area:** Other — Community Power, or Health Justice *if and only if* the application centers access to healthy/safe social support. Avoid claiming to be a health-care, legal-service or domestic-violence agency.

## April–September 2027: one credible investigation

1. **April — outside authority first.** Compensate qualified safeguarding and accessibility reviewers; recruit diverse lived-experience advisors without collecting case histories; map existing services, risks and anti-coercion stop rules. Identify whether a real operator can responsibly host anything.
2. **May — design one door.** Co-design a low-disclosure, paper/phone/browser option that an isolated adult can understand, refuse, leave or contest. Invite criticism before inviting survivor testimony.
3. **June — conditional bounded experiment.** Under independent safeguards and actual informed consent, test one first contact or referral to an existing qualified service; otherwise run synthetic tabletop testing and document why real operation was held.
4. **July — independent audit and repair.** Accessibility and safety review, including device sharing, court orders, contact disclosure, retention, moderation, false reassurance and withdrawal. Review whether the project should exist independently of incumbent services.
5. **August — reusable founding kit.** Publish only approved, de-identified group rules, invitation templates, service-boundary checklists, and independently reviewable process recipes. Make the paper path first-class.
6. **September — owner-governed handoff.** Ask an independent organization or group to critique/adapt the kit on its own terms if willing; report evidence of actual utility, complaints, refusal, costs, limitations and uncompleted gates. No compulsory replication target, partner fiction or survivor data publication.

**The critical test is not number of invitations or active accounts.** It is whether one person without an existing trusted network can access a safe next step **and whether an independent human reviewer can establish what the project actually accomplished**. A second circle may be a later experiment; do not promise a staffed or accredited service in six months.

## Explicit relational-design correspondence and falsifiable source-native demonstration

Voqal's own 2026 fellowship includes **LX Cast's Relational Design Lab**. Its independently public work examines belonging, freedom, responsibility and social/digital design that resists extractive governance. We can **explicitly credit this neighboring inquiry** and perhaps *ask* for independent criticism later. We have **no actual collaboration, affiliation, permission to use the Lab's brand as a partner, agreement for review, or correspondence sent**.

Our distinct contribution is a testable **inheritance-without-inherited-authority** proposition. A public-safe founding practice can be inherited through signed source ancestry while each recipient independently controls its own constitution, refusals and optional published offers. New [RELATIONAL-INHERITANCE-008](./RELATIONAL-INHERITANCE-008.md) uses the **original working WebZ Suitcase signing and founding implementations**, not fake verification stubs, to produce eleven signed packets across four **fictional** key holders. The verifier checks public method digest, A→B→D signed lineage, B and D's separate key-bound GENESIS and operating rules, C's signed refusal, outsider withdrawal rejection, and local withdrawal of only B's own published capability. It uses a separate process for cold replay.

Its claim is strictly `SOFTWARE_CRYPTOGRAPHIC_BOUNDARY_VERIFIED_ONLY`, **not actual transferred human power or belonging**. The fellowship would pay real independent relational/safeguarding reviewers to try to falsify the social assumptions: do communities have actual control of records, funding, physical space, entry/exit, safety and governance, even after the original founder is absent? A successful cryptographic result that fails that human test is a real *failure*, not a validated circle.

For reviewers, this is a useful one-paragraph distinction: **We can already demonstrate that ancestry does not itself confer key authority in our software. We are seeking fellowship support to test whether independently governed practices can meaningfully transfer decision-making to real people who have been excluded from existing networks.** A method can travel; belonging cannot be automated.

## The biggest objection to answer ourselves

**"How can you say 'community-owned' when you don't yet have community co-designers?"** Answer: we cannot claim ownership has already transferred. The fellowship would fund accountable formation: independently compensated participants, power to reject the idea, qualified outside oversight, and genuinely optional local adoption. The founder's lived experience identifies the first problem, but cannot substitute for the community's plural voices.

**"Why is this more than a referral directory?"** Answer: the project's tested object is a **founding protocol**—how people find an initial safe connection, establish specific local rights, record refusals, and create transferable practices they may change—rather than a list of phone numbers. But if an existing service or paper procedure works better, the method should stand down.

**"Why so much technology?"** Answer: the fellowship pays for human work and learning; the current prototypes are contingent, replaceable instruments. A participant can use the first-contact method without a phone, account, internet connection, church, AI system, or 3D printer.

**"Where is the justice dimension?"** Answer: isolate and test a structural barrier to participation, then redistribute decision-making, control over stories, access, and the power to exit—not merely provide a more efficient intake workflow. Examine who remains excluded by language, disability, race, poverty and fear of disclosure without claiming pre-existing equitable outcomes.
