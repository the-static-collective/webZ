The First Human Crossing

Two people, two independently operated computers, one optional source-backed method. A can offer. B may HOLD or REFUSE. Neither side can silently authorize the other.

Field kit, not an execution console. This printable page is an offline checklist only. It never handles keys, performs signatures, or connects to other devices. The trust boundary

What the test can establish

  1. The source data matches pinned Revival material and its attribution.
  2. The signed reLATTE crossing binds to the exact offered bytes.
  3. The receiver's local signed receipts express HOLD or REFUSE.
  4. The source independently checks B's returned receipt signatures against the expected key.

What it cannot establish alone

  1. A particular human's legal identity or agency.
  2. That a real church, venue, or organizer approved an event.
  3. Real-world custody or physical cooperation from a remote JSON file.
  4. Permission to publish, admit, transact, or issue Jubilee credits.

Next external proof requires two actual devices, intentional participants, and observable records of the field session. A GitHub Actions simulation on one runner is not that proof.
