LOCAL BUS NEEDS TWO THINGS NOBODY'S USING

A retired engine computer. A borrowed programming tool. Possibly the smallest repair crew in Minnesota.

A REAL PROBLEM · A POSSIBLE FUTURE · REPAIR NOT COMPLETE

SOUTHERN MINNESOTA — There is a real bus with a real problem. It's a 2008 International BE200 with a 6.4-liter MaxxForce 7 diesel. Its owner reports a failed engine control module (ECM).

The dream is modest: get the bus running again without buying an entirely new pile of equipment.

Somewhere, someone may have a compatible ECM inside a vehicle that has already made its last trip. Somewhere else, a mechanic may have a diagnostic interface sitting on a shelf.

The owner doesn't necessarily need to buy that second tool. They may only need temporary access.

Now imagine two strangers discovering they each hold one missing piece.

One has a part. One has a cable. An old bus has another chance.

That repair hasn't happened.
But the problem is real.

Here's the trick: stop asking only where to buy a solution. Ask what the world already contains, who is caring for it, and what conditions would make sharing possible.

Maybe a scrapyard has a computer that fits. Maybe a diesel technician can lend an interface for an afternoon. Maybe the right person spots the request and corrects a mistaken assumption before anything is shipped.

That's the experiment. Not just fixing one vehicle—finding out whether two voluntary exchanges can turn idle resources into useful work.

And it begins with an honest technical question, because sharing the wrong part would waste everyone's time.

The invitation is real · Two separate asks

Got a retired ECM, or a tool you could lend?

ASK 01 — A possibly compatible salvage ECM. A genuine Navistar/International part for a 2008 MaxxForce 7 would be especially interesting. Please share the part number on the label before offering to remove or ship anything. Compatibility is not confirmed.

I might have an ECM →

ASK 02 — Temporary access to an RP1210 vehicle interface. Navistar has documented NEXIQ USB-Link 1/2, Dearborn DPA 5 and Noregon DLA-series interfaces for its diagnostics software. The required connector, protocol and programming support must be verified for this exact job.

I might lend a tool →

ALSO WELCOME — A diesel technician who can verify the match. A shared engine family isn't evidence of ECU interchangeability. A correct part number and a safe service procedure matter more than an enthusiastic guess.

I know how to check →

What this does not do: It does not reserve, accept, or transfer equipment automatically. No payment or commitment is required to inquire. Please don't publicly post VINs, addresses, keys, firmware, or third-party contact details. We will verify a proposed match privately before arranging custody or use. Do not mail parts unsolicited.

The field notes · Verified / not verified

A good repair begins with a good question.

Known: The owner has a 2008 International BE200 with a MaxxForce 7 (6.4L) and reports the ECM needs replacement. The unit has been removed for assessment.

Known about tools: Navistar's published Engine Diagnostics manual lists the NEXIQ USB-Link 1/2, Dearborn DPA 5 and Noregon DLA among tested interfaces. This is evidence for diagnostics compatibility, not a guarantee of unrestricted firmware flashing or programming authority.

Not yet verified: The current ECM's exact label, which salvaged ECM numbers interchange, whether any Ford vehicle computer will work, interface harness and protocol requirements for bench/vehicle connection, or whether existing firmware and software are authentic and appropriate.

Correction to the rumor: Ford's related 6.4L Power Stroke was offered in 2008–2010 Super Duty trucks, not the F-150. Engine ancestry alone does not establish ECM compatibility. A Ford donor is a lead to investigate, not an approved substitute.

Sources: Navistar Engine Diagnostics user guide, diagnostic interface information · Ford Power Stroke history · 6.4L Super Duty application overview. Source documents describe equipment families; final module selection still requires technical verification.

The machine can make an invitation.
The people decide what crosses.
FIELD EXPERIMENT 001 · BOUNDARY INHABITATION

Be the marble. Check both sides.

From the bus: What precise property of the missing part matters? From the donor: What condition makes an offer reasonable? From the borrowed tool: Is access enough, or is ownership necessary? From the crossing: What must each person agree to before anything moves?

We will record only confirmed steps: exact compatibility checked, willing donor found, loan terms agreed, part actually transferred, and any repair outcome witnessed. An unconfirmed lead stays a lead.
