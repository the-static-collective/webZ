## Why a social-media sprint rather than an announcement blitz?

The next outreach window may be six weeks of social media. This proposal treats social media as **a town-sized listening doorway**, not an engagement farm. Each post invites one particular voluntary conversation; a named human decides whether a specific question should travel to an authorized project steward; a bounded First Yes artifact can be prepared before anyone agrees to participate. A public return follows only from distinct evidence, human review and permission.

The October 22 Waseca County Pioneer coverage says the community-owned grocery effort is pursuing a feasibility study and questions on local sourcing, food access, ownership and involvement: https://wasecacountypioneer.com/news/community-invited-learn-about-grocery-co-op . This premeeting reporting is **not** proof of any outcome at the October 25 meeting, official adoption of this proposal, official social accounts, or current store commitments.

## The 50-minute decision meeting

1. **00–05**: Choose one useful outcome besides likes. Example: real questions answered, one permissioned contribution, one bounded approved next step.
2. **05–12**: Identify who should be heard and who is presently missing. Offer a path for people not on Facebook/Instagram; use printed invitations or an owned site.
3. **12–19**: Inspect accounts and access. Who owns actual official channels? Who may post? Who can correct? Whose private credentials must never be shared?
4. **19–28**: Agree on sources, photo permissions and a human publishing reviewer. Membership count, cost, fundraising, site, suppliers, organizational representation and milestones all require verifiable owner approval.
5. **28–38**: Decide how to respond to questions; route only voluntarily shared, public-safe questions to an actual steward. A commenter is not an enrolled volunteer.
6. **38–45**: Agree on respectful viewpoint-neutral discussion rules, including substantive criticism and a way to correct mistakes; protect people from harassment, doxxing and threats without using those labels to erase disagreement.
7. **45–50**: Assign *the first two drafts* to a real reviewer, agree on an authorized account/publishing path and a date for first weekly review. Any unresolved authority gate remains open.

## Six-week skeleton

- **Oct 26–Nov 1:** Listen to residents; ask what would make grocery shopping useful and which skills people voluntarily enjoy contributing.
- **Nov 2–8:** Explain general co-op principles accurately; ask members what specific local details still require organizer answers.
- **Nov 9–15:** Ask what local producers need; obtain their actual individual participation and photo/quotation permission before tags and endorsements.
- **Nov 16–22:** Explore feasibility and access transparently, never inventing a project status update.
- **Nov 23–29:** Invite a tiny, reversible First Yes task with a willing steward.
- **Nov 30–Dec 6:** Publish **only actually verified** results, corrections and a clear next question; last two draft captions remain `HOLD` until evidence and approval exist.

**Cadence:** two editable drafts per week, twelve total. The app has a one-click CSV export and copy buttons; nothing is posted or scheduled. Facebook Page scheduling is available free through Meta Business Suite with appropriate Page authorization; due to platform scheduling windows, six weeks of editorial planning is not equivalent to six weeks of scheduled posts.

## Public reply → field return

1. Hear a comment as a person, not a lead. Preserve objections and refuse to harvest private hardship in public comments.
2. Paraphrase one general issue locally in the studio's reply desk, and draft a respectful explanation with uncertainty visible.
3. Offer an *optional*, reviewable question, not an assignment. Use the existing `https://garden.abundent.org/workbench/` and `https://kbhqacsdvjsstzyplqij.supabase.co/functions/v1/first-yes` doors when appropriate.
4. The question enters the Garden ledger only through the participant's separate affirmative consent and actual submission. A human owner must accept any task; approved public posts must be checked by the actual editorial owner.
5. Later publication distinguishes **proposed / under review / agreed / tested / self-reported / independently confirmed**. The studio has no access to private inquiries and cannot infer real-world completion.

## Open discussion and First Amendment principles

This is not a legal determination that a private co-op social media page is a government-run public forum. Suggest a consistent published moderation policy that welcomes favorable or unfavorable opinions but prohibits credible threats, harassment, spam, and doxxing; offers corrections and human appeal; and makes clear the difference between user comments and organizational statements. The studio contains a copyable **draft policy**, not one adopted by the co-op.
