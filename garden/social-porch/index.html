A proposal for their next communications meeting

One meeting. Seven decisions.

Suggested 50-minute kickoff. End with an accountable pilot—not just a calendar of posts.

  1. 00–05 MINWhat's social media actually for?

    Pick one outcome: real questions, opt-in participants, clear answers, or approved next steps—not just impressions.

  2. 05–12 MINWho are we inviting to speak?

    Neighbors, potential owners, producers, and people who have concerns. Decide language, accessibility and where off-platform participation goes.

  3. 12–19 MINWho owns the voice?

    Verify actual pages, admin access, factual reviewer, final publisher and backup. Don't assume the person designing posts can speak for the co-op.

  4. 19–28 MINWhat can we safely say?

    Approve plain-language facts, photograph/quote permissions, unknown answers, and a review rule for dates, finance, membership and partnerships.

  5. 28–38 MINWhat does each reply become?

    Define listening, triage, permission to route a question, ownership, and one bounded First Yes artifact. No automatic volunteer enrollment.

  6. 38–45 MINHow do we handle disagreement?

    Welcome fair criticism, correct errors, avoid personal details in comments; publish narrowly tailored moderation rules for threats, harassment, doxxing and spam.

  7. 45–50 MINWho approves the first two posts?

    Assign a publisher, reviewer, response owner, first post, due date and one-week check-in. Record what is still unresolved.

Twelve draft posts / zero scheduled posts

Ready-to-edit conversation starters.

The first ten are independent discussion drafts; the last two are evidence-gated templates. Every caption needs the actual publisher's editorial review. When publishing independently, always disclose the affiliation clearly.

The real innovation / a comment can become a useful act

A response desk, not a comment trap.

Translate a *general, public-safe theme* into a respectful reply and a suggested human-reviewed next question. This tool does not scrape comments or store anything. Don't paste names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, personal difficulties, or private messages.

The reply is a draft for an independent community listener, not a response issued by an official cooperative representative. Actual comments deserve attentive human reading, not automatic sorting or automated promises.

1 · A person speaks

Listen and answer the part you know. Don't collect private hardship stories in a public thread.

2 · One specific question emerges

Prepare a shareable, anonymized draft; ask whether the person wants it passed to an actual authorized steward.

3 · A real person decides

If accepted, prepare one useful artifact and report what happened. If declined, record that without pushing again.
