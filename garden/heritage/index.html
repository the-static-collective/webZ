AN ARCHAEOLOGY OF ABUNDANCE

Things worth
bringing back.

Human beings have always invented more than grocery shelves. We made markets, ovens, seed drawers, common meals, recovery routes and places where everybody knew somebody who could help. Which parts should our next grocery remember?

01 / The shape of a place

A grocery can be three places at once.

The best things in food history were never only about moving goods. They let people gather, discover, preserve skills and make arrangements with one another. The Garden can start restoring those capacities at a size that fits the people actually willing to help.

A FIELD MEMORY / A DESIGN SEED

The barrel of beans.

At a Waseca grocery meeting, one attendee described the pleasure of plunging a hand into a barrel of dried beans. The same person learned of the gathering through their hairstylist. Those details are recalled by a participant, not independently recorded testimony.

Another meeting participant described a food spread in a photograph as the work of Infinity Catering. This is an attributed recollection, not a catering partnership or permission to reproduce the photograph.

Our proposal: a small, abundant seasonal display of three or four carefully served samples, cards from consenting growers and cooks, and one invitation: “What do you remember loving about the places your community gathered?”

A touch-only display of non-sale beans could honor the memory; edible samples must be handled separately and safely.

03 / One small experiment first

Four weeks of remembering.

A proposed rotating series for one willing host and appropriately permitted food handlers. Start with a paper/mock display if a physical food service is not approved. Each week asks what matters, who might help, and what should change.

WEEK 01 / THE BARREL

Touch.

A carefully labeled tactile nonfood bean bin, sealed sale samples, the honest scale and people's co-op memories.

WEEK 02 / THE BREAD

Learn.

With an authorized baker or a photo-only trial, bring one family bread recipe into conversation.

WEEK 03 / THE SEED

Grow.

Show a seed drawer, regional planting notes and the faces of people who enjoy teaching gardeners.

WEEK 04 / THE HARVEST

Return.

Explore safe preserving knowledge and ask how surplus food might be recovered by willing partners.

Every week, one question: What do you remember loving that you wish existed here again? Gather responses voluntarily, separate anecdotes from commitments, and return only reviewed public learning to the community.

04 / Permission is part of hospitality

Beautiful, and responsible.

Before live samples, a real host must approve the site and determine the applicable Minnesota food handling requirements. A photograph, user suggestion or website does not authorize a caterer, farmer, cooperative or facility to participate.

  • Food: ingredients and major allergens visible; sanitary portions and correct temperatures; responsible handlers.
  • Rules: Minnesota Statutes § 28A.151 regulates qualifying farmers-market/community-event sampling; applicability must be confirmed for each venue and format.
  • People: personal memories and identities remain private unless the person approves a specific public quotation.
  • Work: ideas → offers → permission → a small trial → a confirmed return. No inferred commitments.
